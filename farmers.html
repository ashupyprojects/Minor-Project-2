👨‍🌾 Farmers Schemes

PM-Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN)

This scheme provides small and marginal farmers with direct income support of ₹6,000 per year, paid in three equal installments of ₹2,000 each. The payments go directly into the beneficiary’s bank account via DBT (Direct Benefit Transfer). It aims to supplement farmers’ income for agriculture and allied activities and reduce their dependence on informal credit. Launched in December 2018, the scheme covers all land-holding farmers subject to inclusion criteria, and uses Aadhaar-based verification and digitised infrastructure to ensure transparency, timely payments and benefits to intended recipients.

Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)

PMFBY is a crop-insurance programme that provides protection to farmers against yield losses from natural calamities, pests and diseases. It works on an area-approach basis covering most food, oilseed and annual commercial/horticultural crops. Farmers pay a minimal premium while the government subsidises the rest; claims are settled quickly and benefits passed directly. The scheme reduces distress sale of crops, promotes timely sowing and harvesting, and protects the agricultural value chain. It involves multiple stakeholders including state governments, banks and insurance companies to ensure wide coverage and effective support for farmers.

Kisan Credit Card (KCC)

The Kisan Credit Card scheme offers farmers timely and affordable credit for crop production, post-harvest operations and allied activities under a simple unified system. Introduced in 1998, it provides a revolving credit facility, minimal documentation, and flexible repayment terms. Many banks also issue RuPay KCC cards for easy access to funds. Interest subventions and prompt-repayment incentives are additional benefits. This scheme helps reduce dependence on informal lenders and supports smooth agricultural operations by providing working capital when it’s needed most.

PM Krishi Sinchai Yojana (PMKSY)

PMKSY aims to achieve the vision of “Har Khet Ko Pani” — giving every agricultural field access to adequate irrigation and improving water-use efficiency. It incorporates micro-irrigation, watershed development, and other interventions to expand irrigated area and optimise water resources. By improving irrigation coverage, the scheme increases productivity, resilience to drought and enhances cropping intensity. It also supports efficient water management technologies and infrastructure to ensure sustainable agriculture in water-scarce regions, thereby contributing to higher incomes and agriculture sector stability.

National Agriculture Market (e-NAM)

e-NAM is India’s pan-India electronic trading platform for agricultural commodities, launched in April 2016. It links existing agricultural produce market committees (APMCs) and creates a unified digital marketplace for farmers, buyers and traders. The scheme enables online bidding, real-time price discovery, transparent transactions, and quick payments to farmers. It helps reduce intermediaries, inefficiencies and market fragmentation, thereby improving farmers’ returns and access to multiple markets across states. e-NAM supports the broader goal of modernising agriculture marketing infrastructure in India.
