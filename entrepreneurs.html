Start-Up India

Start-Up India is a flagship initiative launched on 16 January 2016 to build a strong ecosystem for nurturing innovation and startups in the country. It offers simplified compliance, funding support via a “Fund of Funds”, tax exemptions for eligible years, and connects startups with incubators, investors, and mentors. Through the Startup India Hub, it provides hand-holding support from idea to scale-up. The program targets large-scale job creation, fosters entrepreneurship across sectors including agriculture and manufacturing, and empowers youth with innovation-driven opportunities.

Stand-Up India

Stand-Up India was launched on 5 April 2016 to promote entrepreneurship among women and SC/ST entrepreneurs by facilitating bank loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore for greenfield enterprises in manufacturing, services or trading. Each branch of a Scheduled Commercial Bank is tasked to provide at least one loan to a woman entrepreneur and one to an SC/ST borrower. The scheme supports inclusive growth by ensuring that traditionally under-represented communities gain access to credit, mentorship and hand-holding support.

MSME Loan Scheme

The MSME Loan Scheme, implemented through the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE), provides collateral-free credit to micro and small enterprises (MSEs) across India. Launched by the Ministry of MSME and SIDBI, the scheme encourages banks and financial institutions to lend up to ₹2 crore without requiring third-party guarantees. It covers manufacturing, service, and trading units to promote entrepreneurship, especially among first-generation business owners. The government shares a guarantee of up to 85% of the loan amount, ensuring risk coverage for lenders and improved access to finance for small enterprises to grow, modernize, and generate employment.

Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY)

PMMY, launched 8 April 2015, aims to provide micro-credit up to ₹10 lakh to non-corporate, non-farm small/micro enterprises in manufacturing, trading and services. It segments loans as Shishu (≤ ₹50k), Kishor (₹50k-₹5 lakh) and Tarun (₹5-₹10 lakh). The scheme enables new or existing units, encourages entrepreneurship among youth and women, and reduces dependency on informal credit. Through MUDRA Bank and partner institutions, it supports livelihood generation, employment creation and scaling up of micro-enterprises.

Digital India Initiatives

Digital India is a transformational programme to enhance India’s digital infrastructure, digital literacy and delivery of government services electronically. It focuses on connecting rural areas with high-speed internet, promoting e-governance and enabling digital platforms for citizens, businesses and governments. By integrating technology, the initiative supports start-ups, MSMEs and employs innovation in public services like health, education and financial inclusion. Digital India fosters economic growth, job creation and inclusive access to digital resources across the country.
