PM-Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN)

Launched in February 2019, Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN) is a Central Sector Scheme aimed at providing income support to all landholding farmers across the country. Under this scheme, eligible farmers receive ₹6,000 per year, payable in three equal installments of ₹2,000 each, directly into their bank accounts through Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT). The main objective of PM-KISAN is to ensure a steady source of income for small and marginal farmers to meet their agricultural and domestic needs. The scheme benefits over 12 crore farmers and helps reduce their dependency on informal credit sources. Eligibility criteria include ownership of cultivable land, while institutional landholders and government employees are excluded. The scheme has improved financial inclusion and transparency in farmer welfare distribution. PM-KISAN has become a major step towards ensuring farmer empowerment, supplementing agricultural investment, and supporting the government’s vision of “Doubling Farmers’ Income.”

Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)

Launched in 2016, PMFBY is a comprehensive crop insurance scheme that provides financial protection to farmers against crop loss due to natural calamities, pests, or diseases. It replaced earlier schemes like NAIS and MNAIS, offering a uniform low premium — 2% for Kharif, 1.5% for Rabi, and 5% for commercial and horticultural crops. The scheme aims to stabilize farmers’ income, encourage adoption of modern farming practices, and ensure food security. The remaining premium is subsidized by the government (Centre and States). PMFBY uses technology-based assessments — remote sensing, drones, and GPS — to ensure transparency in crop loss estimation. Claims are settled directly to farmers’ accounts. By protecting farmers from unexpected losses, PMFBY reduces distress and enhances agricultural risk resilience. It’s one of the world’s largest crop insurance programs by coverage.

Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)

Launched in 2015, PMKSY aims to achieve the vision of “Har Khet Ko Paani” (Water to Every Field) and improve water-use efficiency in agriculture through “Per Drop More Crop”. The scheme promotes efficient irrigation practices, micro-irrigation, and water conservation techniques to increase productivity and sustainability. It integrates multiple programs such as Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme (AIBP), Watershed Development, and On-Farm Water Management (OFWM). PMKSY focuses on creating water sources, repairing traditional water bodies, and ensuring equitable water distribution. Implementation is done in coordination with state governments. This scheme plays a vital role in combating water scarcity, reducing dependence on monsoon rainfall, and ensuring climate-resilient agriculture. Overall, PMKSY strengthens irrigation infrastructure, enhances crop intensity, and supports sustainable agricultural growth.

e-NAM (National Agriculture Market)

Launched in April 2016, e-NAM is a pan-India electronic trading portal that connects existing Agricultural Produce Market Committees (APMCs) to create a unified national market for agricultural commodities. The platform promotes transparent price discovery, online trading, and better market access for farmers. It integrates over 1,000 mandis across 18 states and 3 UTs, helping farmers sell their produce anywhere in India. Farmers can view real-time price data, compare offers, and get fair prices without middlemen. The platform supports digital payments and quality certification, ensuring efficiency and transparency. e-NAM also helps in reducing marketing costs, improving supply chain efficiency, and strengthening agri value chains. It is a major reform towards “One Nation, One Market”, enhancing farmers’ income and promoting digital agriculture in India.

Soil Health Card Scheme

Launched in 2015, the Soil Health Card Scheme aims to promote balanced use of fertilizers by providing farmers with detailed information on the nutrient status of their soil. Under this program, soil samples are tested in laboratories, and Soil Health Cards are issued once every two years, containing crop-wise recommendations for fertilizers and soil amendments. The objective is to ensure judicious use of inputs, improve soil fertility, and enhance agricultural productivity sustainably. This scheme has covered over 14 crore farmers across India, reducing excessive fertilizer use and promoting awareness about soil conservation. By improving soil health management, it contributes to long-term sustainability and supports the government’s goal of environmentally responsible farming. In short, it empowers farmers with scientific knowledge to make informed decisions for better yields and reduced input costs.

Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY)

Introduced in 2007, Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) aims to promote holistic agricultural growth by allowing states the flexibility to plan and execute development programs based on local needs. It encourages states to increase investment in agriculture and allied sectors to achieve 4% growth in the sector during the 11th Five-Year Plan and beyond. The scheme supports projects in crop development, horticulture, dairy, fisheries, marketing, and infrastructure. Under RKVY-RAFTAAR (Remunerative Approaches for Agriculture and Allied Sector Rejuvenation), launched in 2017, the focus is on innovation, startups, and entrepreneurship in the agri sector. The scheme has enhanced state-level planning, resource mobilization, and technology adoption. Overall, RKVY plays a crucial role in modernizing agriculture, improving rural livelihoods, and promoting food and nutritional security through coordinated efforts between the Centre and states.
